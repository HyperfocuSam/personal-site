# What the 2026 Budget's HK$50 Million AI Training Push Actually Means for Your Company

Hong Kong's 2026-27 Budget earmarked HK$50 million for AI training, and the headlines called it a bold push for citywide adoption. If you run HR or L&D and are trying to work out whether any of that money reaches your team, the honest answer is: almost none of it, and the schemes that do help were not in the speech.

I have delivered AI training to 10,000+ professionals across 70+ organizations, including a major Hong Kong bank, a major Hong Kong utility, and PolyU. Here is what the measures mean in practice, what they miss, and what to do regardless.

## What the budget actually includes

**HK$50 million for AI literacy.** The government will invite public organizations to partner with tech companies and universities on AI courses, seminars, and competitions for students, young people, and the general public. Awareness-level training, not corporate workforce development.

**Upskill Hong Kong.** The Employees Retraining Board is being upgraded from retraining the unemployed to upskilling the employed, with AI as a core strand. This is the measure closest to companies, and its courses will be general-purpose and slow to appear.

**HK$100 million for government AI transformation.** An AI Efficacy Enhancement Team will coordinate across departments. It does not help private companies directly, but it signals that the government takes internal adoption seriously.

**HK$2 billion for digital education in schools.** AI programs for primary and secondary students. A long-term workforce pipeline, not an immediate corporate impact.

**HK$10 billion Innovation and Technology Industry-Oriented Fund.** Market capital for emerging domains including AI and robotics. Investment funding, not training funding.

**Committee on AI+ and Industry Development Strategy.** Chaired by Financial Secretary Paul Chan, with an initial focus on life and health technology and embodied AI. This committee will shape AI policy for the next several years.

## What's missing

The HK$50 million is for public AI literacy, not corporate training. If you are looking for government money to train your team, the picture is thinner than the headlines suggest.

**TVP is gone.** The Technology Voucher Programme, which many companies used to fund technology adoption projects including training, stopped accepting applications after December 2024. It has not been renewed.

**NITTP is the one direct training subsidy.** The New Industrialisation and Technology Training Programme, formerly RTTP, matches course fees 1:1 up to HK$250,000 per enterprise per financial year, for courses registered with the VTC. It is the only scheme that pays for training as training. Details in [the buyer's guide](/blog/how-to-choose-ai-training-hong-kong).

**BUD is the closest replacement, if you read the caps carefully.** The BUD Fund's cumulative ceiling is HK$7 million per company; the HK$150,000 figure often quoted is the per-application cap on the fast-track Easy BUD route. BUD funds branding, upgrading, and market-development projects, so training can sit inside a funded project but not stand alone. Since 15 June 2026 it carries targeted support for projects with AI elements.

**Beyond NITTP, no broad corporate AI training subsidy.** Unlike Singapore's SkillsFuture, Hong Kong's 2026 measures fund public literacy and institutional programs, not private-sector workforce development.

Industry groups have called HK$50 million "far from adequate," and the arithmetic supports them. One engagement I ran for a major Hong Kong bank put 1,530 staff through five sessions. HK$50 million spread across a city is a signal of intent, not a training budget.

## What this means for your company

**Don't wait for government programs.** Upskill Hong Kong courses will take time to develop and will be generic by design. If you want training on your own workflows, you are paying for it, and NITTP halves the bill if the course is registered.

**Start with behavior change, not tool awareness.** The HK$50 million is aimed at helping people understand what AI is. Most corporate teams are past that. They have seen the demos. What they need is workflow integration — taking the tasks they do every day and redesigning them with AI — which means [hands-on training on their actual work, not public seminars](/blog/why-ai-training-doesnt-stick).

**Use the policy signal internally.** Even though the budget does not fund your training, it gives you cover. "The government is putting HK$50 million into AI training and the Financial Secretary is chairing a committee on it" is a useful sentence in your next L&D budget proposal.

**Start smaller than you think.** The programs I have seen work began with one department and 10 to 20 people, not a company-wide rollout. I wrote up [how one of those was designed](/blog/how-to-design-ai-pioneer-program). By the time the second department asked for it, the business case had been made by the first.

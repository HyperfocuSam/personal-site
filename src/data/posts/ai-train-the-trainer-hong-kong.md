# How to Choose an AI Train-the-Trainer Program in Hong Kong

Hong Kong companies are asking for more AI training than there are people who can deliver it properly. I say that from inside the problem - the last two years of corporate delivery took me from Yau Tong to LA, and finding trainers who can do this work is harder than finding clients who want it.

If you already train, consult, or teach, that gap is your way in.

The missing piece usually isn't AI knowledge. It's the jump from using AI yourself to running a room of 30 skeptical professionals through it. That jump is what a train-the-trainer program is supposed to sell you. Whether it actually does depends on what's inside, and the differences between programs are big.

## What train-the-trainer means, literally

A train-the-trainer (TTT) program takes people who can already teach and equips them to deliver a specific curriculum. You're not buying AI lessons. You're buying someone else's delivery system: the materials, the exercise flow, the answers to the questions participants always ask, and ideally the right to deliver it commercially.

The honest version of the pitch: you skip two years of building and breaking workshop content, and start from what already works.

## The five things a program should hand you

I've watched trainers walk into their first paid session. Charisma doesn't decide whether they survive it. Five things do:

1. **Complete materials, not sample slides.** The full deck, the exercises, the handouts, in a form you can adapt. If you're renting locked content, ask what happens when a client wants changes.
2. **A delivery structure.** Timing, sequencing, what to do when a room goes quiet. The run-of-show matters more than the slides.
3. **A facilitation method.** How to run hands-on exercises where 30 people all hit different problems at once. Most programs skip this part, and it's the part that breaks first-timers.
4. **A failure-recovery plan.** AI tools break live. A model updates overnight, a demo stops working mid-session. What you do in that moment decides whether you lose the room. If the program never discusses this, it was built by people who don't deliver.
5. **A path to actual work.** Certification is paper. Ask what happens after: a trainer community, referred engagements, delivery support - or does the relationship end at the cert?

## Questions to ask before you pay

Take these to any provider, including us:

- Who delivered a corporate session with these exact materials in the last month?
- Can I observe a real session before committing?
- Do I keep the materials if I leave the network?
- What happens when a client asks for content you haven't built yet?
- How many of your certified trainers actually deliver paid work?

The pattern behind all five: separate the programs run by practitioners from the programs run by certificate printers.

## Where I stand in this

I co-founded Adaptig and run its train-the-trainer program, so discount everything above accordingly - I'm describing the checklist I built our program to pass.

What I'll say for it: the methodology comes from delivering to 10,000+ professionals across 70+ organizations, and the trainer network delivers in 20+ countries. The materials trainers get are the ones I use in bank and enterprise rooms myself, updated whenever a session teaches us something.

The checklist still works against anyone, us included. Ask us the same questions.

## Who shouldn't do this

If you want slides to read aloud, no TTT program will save the session. AI training is hands-on or it's nothing - participants open the tools, and things go wrong in real time. If that sounds stressful rather than fun, sell something else.

If it sounds fun: the demand is real, Hong Kong is short of people who can do this properly, and most of the hard content work is already done for you. Come see a session first.
